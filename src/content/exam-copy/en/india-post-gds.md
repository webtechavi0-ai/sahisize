India Post GDS is one of the few forms that states its rules in **shapes**, not just sizes. The photo is **4:5** and the signature is **5:2**. If you crop a photo to a square or a signature to a long thin strip, it will not match, even when the KB is right.

### The numbers from the Schedule-II 2026 notice

- **Photo:** JPG, recommended 320×400 px, 30–100 KB, 72–150 DPI, plain white or light background, face filling 70–80% of the image, no shadows or red-eye.
- **Signature:** JPG, recommended 300×120 px, 20–100 KB, 72–150 DPI, black or blue pen on white paper, signature filling 70–80% of the image, no borders or extra marks, no typed or digital signature.

SahiSize writes 150 DPI, the top of the allowed range.

### Doing it on a phone

1. Sit facing a window. Hold the phone at eye level, about an arm's length away.
2. In SahiSize, crop so your head and the top of your shoulders fill the 4:5 frame. The face should take most of the space; this is tighter than a typical selfie.
3. Sign on white paper with a dark pen. Sign slowly; the notice warns against shaky or pixelated signatures.
4. Crop close to the ink in the 5:2 frame so it covers 70–80% of the image. Clean signature removes paper edges and shadows, which count as "extra marks".
5. Save both files and pick them from your Downloads folder in the form.

### What you do not upload

GDS selection is based on the Class 10 marks you type into the form. There is no certificate upload at the application stage; documents are checked later.

### Timing

India Post runs several schedules a year. In 2026, Schedule-II opened in late August. Forms have usually appeared around January and August.

### If the portal still rejects it

A tight 320×400 crop can save under 30 KB; Auto-fit raises the quality to cross it. If the signature is refused, check for a visible paper edge or a shadow.
