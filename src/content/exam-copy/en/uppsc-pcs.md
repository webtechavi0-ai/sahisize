UPPSC does something few other commissions do: the "photo" upload is not a plain photo. It is a **composite**. You paste your passport photo on white paper, sign just below it, and scan the two together as one image. A plain face photo in that box is a common mistake.

### The two uploads

| Upload | Physical size | Pixels at 200 DPI | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Photo with signature below | 5 cm wide × 6 cm high | about 394×472 | up to 50 KB |
| Signature (separate) | 6 cm wide × 3 cm high | about 472×236 | up to 30 KB |

The pixel values are worked out from the centimetre sizes at 200 DPI. Sources give only a maximum, not a minimum.

### Step by step

1. Print or get a recent passport-size photo with a light background.
2. Glue it near the top of a plain white sheet.
3. Sign in black ink right under the photo. Use your **full running signature**, not initials.
4. On another part of the sheet, sign again for the separate signature upload. It should match the one under the photo.
5. Photograph the sheet flat in daylight, or scan it.
6. In SahiSize, crop the photo-plus-signature area for the first step. The crop is locked to the 5×6 cm shape, and Auto-fit keeps it under 50 KB.
7. Crop only the second signature for the signature step. Clean signature whitens the paper and keeps it well under 30 KB.

### OTR comes first

UPPSC now uses One Time Registration. The images you upload there carry over to later UPPSC exams, so it is worth getting them right once rather than fixing them in every form.

### Timing

The PCS form has usually opened around February–March. Check the UPPSC site for the current notice before you start.

### If the portal still rejects it

Look at three things: is the signature visible under the photo, is the file under the KB cap, and is it a JPG rather than a PNG or PDF. These sizes come from secondary guides, so if the form shows a different limit, follow the form and change the KB in custom size.
