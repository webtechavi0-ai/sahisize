Banking forms (IBPS, SBI, RBI, NABARD), NEET, HTET, DSSSB and IAF Agniveervayu ask for a **thumb impression** upload. Most people have never made one at home. A smudged black blob is the usual result — and smudged impressions get rejected.

Here is how to make a clean one in about five minutes, and how to get it to the exact size your exam wants.

## What you need

- A **stamp ink pad** (black or blue). Any stationery shop has one for about ₹30–50.
- A **plain white A4 sheet** — printer paper, no lines.
- Tissue or a cloth to wipe your thumb.
- Your phone, and daylight or a bright room light.

No ink pad? A blue or black ballpoint refill rubbed on the thumb works in a pinch, but an ink pad gives a much cleaner print.

## Which thumb?

Banking notices ask for the **left thumb**. If you do not have a left thumb, they allow the right one — the rule is in the notice. NEET is different: it asks for **all fingers and thumbs of both hands**. DSSSB asks for **left and right thumb as two separate files**. Read your exam's rules first.

## Step 1: Make the impression

1. Wash and fully dry your hands. Oily skin smudges.
2. Press your thumb **lightly** on the ink pad. Dab two or three times — do not soak it.
3. Check the thumb: the ridges should be inked, not a wet layer of ink.
4. Place the thumb flat on the paper and press **straight down** with gentle, firm pressure. Count to two.
5. **Lift straight up.** Do not roll or slide — that is what causes smudges.
6. Make 3–4 impressions on the sheet, with space between them. You will pick the best one.
7. Let the ink dry for a minute before you take the photo.

A good impression shows clear ridge lines across the whole thumb tip. If it is a solid dark patch, you used too much ink. If it is patchy and pale, use a bit more.

## Step 2: Photograph it

- Keep the paper flat on a table near a window.
- Hold the phone directly above, parallel to the paper.
- Turn flash **off** and make sure no shadow falls on the print.
- Get close enough that the impression fills a good part of the screen, then tap to focus.
- Take two or three shots and pick the sharpest.

You do not need a scanner. A modern phone camera captures ridges well if the light is even.

## The NEET finger impressions

For NEET you need all ten prints. The usual way is:

1. On one A4 sheet, make the four fingers of the left hand in a row, then the left thumb next to them.
2. Below that, do the same for the right hand.
3. Write "Left hand" and "Right hand" above each row in small letters, if the form or bulletin asks for labels.
4. Photograph the whole sheet in one shot, flat and sharp.

Check the NEET form for whether it wants one image or separate images per hand, as the layout has changed between years.

## Common mistakes

- **Too much ink.** The ridges fill in and the print becomes a blob. Dab lightly.
- **Rolling the thumb.** It smears the edges. Press and lift straight.
- **Lined paper or coloured paper.** Use plain white.
- **Shadow of the phone on the print.** Move the light or the phone.
- **Photo of a photo.** Never photograph a print shown on another screen.
- **Wrong hand.** Banking wants the left thumb unless you do not have one.

## FAQ

### Can I use blue ink?

Yes for banking — the notice says black or blue. NEET also allows black or blue. Black usually comes out a little crisper after compression.

### My file is only 12 KB but IBPS wants 20–50 KB. What now?

A clean thumb print on white paper compresses very well. SahiSize's preset raises quality and, if needed, adds invisible padding so the file reaches 20 KB without changing how it looks.

### Can I make the thumb impression with a mobile app or fingerprint sensor?

No. Exams want an ink impression on paper. A phone fingerprint sensor does not produce an image you can upload.

### Do I need the same thumb impression at the exam centre?

Biometric thumb or finger scans are often taken at the centre and during document verification. Your uploaded impression should be a genuine one of your own thumb.
